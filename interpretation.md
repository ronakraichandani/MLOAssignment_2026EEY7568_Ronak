## Interpolation
The tree reaches exactly 100.0% training accuracy on clean CIFAR-10 (50,000 images) and on CIFAR-10 with 15% flipped labels, and 99.99% / 99.95% on clean / noisy Cats-vs-Dogs (20k images). The two Cats-vs-Dogs trees stopped 1-10 samples short because they hit the max_depth=8 cap, so their guarantee of exact interpolation was cut off by the cap, not by a flaw in the construction.
On clean CIFAR-10 the root already fits 98.0% of the training set, so the tree is tiny (10 nodes, depth 1, 0.55M parameters). With 15% label noise the root fits only 80.8%, and the tree grows to 20 nodes (depth 2, 1.5M parameters) to memorise the flipped labels: it fits 100% of the noisy labels, i.e. 100% of the flipped samples, but then agrees with the true labels on only 84.8% of the training set. Cats-vs-Dogs is the hard case: the root fits 89% (clean) and 83% (noisy), and the trees need 15 and 22 nodes at depth 8, mostly linear (width-0) nodes.

## Generalisation and double descent
Model-wise (width-k ResNet-18, 15k CIFAR-10 images, 15% label noise, 120 epochs, single seed), training error reaches about 0 at k=6 (about 100k parameters). Test error at the end of training is 0.689 (k=1), 0.665 (k=2), 0.689 (k=4), 0.663 (k=6), 0.656 (k=8), then falls steadily to 0.625 (k=12), 0.591 (k=24), 0.543 (k=32), 0.507 (k=48) and 0.476 (k=64). So there is only a weak bump at k=4, right where training error leaves the underfitting regime (0.51 to 0.09). I would not call this a clear double-descent peak: the bump is about 2 points and a single seed cannot separate it from noise. What we do see clearly is the second descent: beyond the interpolation threshold, wider is monotonically better, even though every model memorises all the noisy labels.
Epoch-wise, the effect is large and clean. Comparing the final test error with the best test error seen during training, the overfitting penalty is +0.1 points at k=1, +9.0 points at k=6, +7.5 at k=8, +3.9 at k=16, +1.9 at k=24 and only +0.3 at k=64. Mid-sized models first generalise well and then deteriorate as they memorise the noise (the classical early-stopping picture), and this penalty shrinks as the model grows. This is the over-parameterised side of double descent seen along the training-time axis, and it is what the model-wise curve hides because only end-of-training numbers are plotted there.
Tree-wise (8k images, 15% noise, fixed node width W, 800 optimiser steps per node), all trees interpolate (100%), and test accuracy is 42.1% (W=0, 437 nodes, 0.23M parameters), 41.3% (W=8), 42.6% (W=32), 44.5% (W=128, 11 nodes) and 47.0% (W=512, a single node, i.e. just a wide MLP). Smaller nodes mean deeper, bigger trees and slightly worse accuracy, so the more of the memorisation work is done by one wide network instead of many repairs, the better the generalisation. A caveat: the single wide MLP of width 128 (67k parameters, 3000-step budget in the baseline vs 800 in the tree) interpolates with a smaller parameter count than any interpolating tree, so the tree is not a size-efficient way to interpolate. The two budgets differ, so this comparison is not perfectly controlled.
Why does the tree generalise worse than its root? Under noise, the root has 63.5% test accuracy and the full tree 58.6% (CIFAR-10); on Cats-vs-Dogs clean 75.8% to 72.0%. The children are trained only to fix training errors, many of which are flipped labels or hard outliers, and their closed-form weights are large (they must override the parent's logit gap). Any test point that happens to trigger a child is pushed towards an idiosyncratic class. The tree adds capacity exactly where the data is unreliable. On clean CIFAR-10 the damage is negligible (70.5% to 70.4%) because there are only 10 small repair nodes.

## Receptive fields
Gradient (SmoothGrad) receptive fields of root class logits and of repair nodes are diffuse: the 50% gradient mass spreads over about 39-45% of the pixels, normalised entropy is 0.99+ for all nodes, and the centre-of-mass offset is small (0.01-0.04 of the half-diagonal), i.e. objects are centred and the nodes look at the whole image with a slight bias to the centre. There is no clear trend with depth: root nodes and depth-1, 2 and 3 repair nodes have statistically similar spread. The activation-maximisation images are high-frequency, adversarial-looking textures; only a few show structure (vertical stripes for deer and horse, diagonal stripes for frog), so they are weak evidence about what a node 'looks for'. The top-activating real images, in contrast, share strong background and colour cues (blue sky for airplanes, green grass for deer, frogs and birds), which suggests that, with a frozen low-capacity trunk, nodes lean on global colour and background statistics rather than object parts. Repair nodes do not seem more local than the roots; they look at the same global features but in a slightly different direction of feature space, which is what lets them carve out individual samples. ## Size minimisation
The first lever is the per-node minimum-width search (linear, 16, 64, 256 hidden units): it keeps most nodes as small as possible, and on Cats-vs-Dogs noisy 15 of 22 nodes are plain linear classifiers. The second lever is pruning children with few positives. On clean CIFAR-10, removing children with fewer than 200 positives cuts parameters from 553k to 430k (-22%) while train accuracy drops 100% to 99.6% and test accuracy stays 70.4-70.5%. On Cats-vs-Dogs, pruning from 15 to 5 nodes shrinks 703k to 560k parameters with no loss at all, and for the noisy tree 584k to 560k (22 to 5 nodes) with 0.1 points less training accuracy and the same test accuracy. Most of the remaining parameters sit in the few large nodes (the root and the wide children), so further savings must come from them. Magnitude sparsification without fine-tuning is fragile: 50% sparsity loses 2.6 points of train accuracy, 80% sparsity collapses to about 53-66%, because the closed-form weights assume that the children are perfect. Therefore: pruning is effective, sparsification needs retraining. (The "full" baseline train accuracy in minimize.json is 99.99% rather than 100% because the cached features were float16; the code now stores float32.)

## Repair depth as a noise detector
The repair depth (how deep in the tree a sample needed a repair) is an informative signal of label noise on CIFAR-10: AUROC 0.75, with 7% of root-correct samples being noisy versus 45% at depth 1. On Cats-vs-Dogs noisy the AUROC is only 0.56, because the binary problem is hard on clean data too (many clean hard examples get repaired as well).

## Conclusions
The tree construction does what it promises: interpolation is guaranteed up to the depth cap, and the nodes can be very small. But interpolation by repair is not a good route to generalisation: the repair nodes fit noise and hard examples, test accuracy drops (by up to 5 points) when labels are noisy, and a single wide MLP interpolates with fewer parameters than any tree we grew. Double descent appears here mainly as epoch-wise overfitting that fades with width and as a monotone second descent in the model-wise curve; a clear sharp peak was not observed with our single seed and 15k training images. Limitations: single seed everywhere; frozen trunk trained for only 3 epochs on noisy labels; Cats-vs-Dogs trees truncated by depth 8; the tree-wise comparison uses unequal step budgets and 8k images; receptive-field analysis is qualitative on a limited number of nodes. Natural next steps are a final fine-tuning of the weights (instead of closed-form), multiple seeds, and intervals around the double-descent curve.
